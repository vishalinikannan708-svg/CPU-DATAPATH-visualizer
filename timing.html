Timing Analysis

Timing analysis explains how digital signals change with respect to time. It helps us understand clock pulses, signal transitions, propagation delay and the correct synchronization of digital operations.

Why Timing Matters

In a digital system, signals must reach the required components at the correct time. Timing ensures that data is sampled, processed and transferred in a synchronized manner. A small timing difference can affect the correctness and reliability of a digital circuit.

Clock

The clock provides regular timing pulses that synchronize operations in a digital circuit.

Frequency

Frequency represents the number of clock cycles occurring in one second and is measured in Hertz.

Period

The clock period is the time required to complete one complete clock cycle.

Rising Edge

The rising edge occurs when a signal changes from logic LOW to logic HIGH.

Falling Edge

The falling edge occurs when a signal changes from logic HIGH to logic LOW.

Propagation Delay

Propagation delay is the time taken for a change at the input of a circuit to appear at its output.

Important Timing Parameters

Setup Time

Setup time is the minimum amount of time that input data must remain stable before the active clock edge.

Hold Time

Hold time is the minimum amount of time that input data must remain stable after the active clock edge.

Clock Period

Clock period represents the duration of one complete clock cycle and is related to frequency using T = 1 / f.

Propagation Delay

Propagation delay represents the time required for a signal transition to travel through a logic component.
